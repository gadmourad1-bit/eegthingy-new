Dear Editor:

We submit "An End-to-End Motor-Imagery Brain–Computer Interface: From Low-Cost
Acquisition and Deployed Classical Decoding to a Reproducible Benchmark of
Compact Coordinate-Field Networks" for consideration as a Regular Paper in
TBME.

The manuscript documents a complete motor-imagery BCI chain: a 16-channel
consumer-grade acquisition rig with scripted cue presentation, an online
pipeline combining aligned classical decoders with unsupervised in-context
calibration and temporal evidence gating (91% within-participant and 86%
participant-independent accuracy on the local cohort, with completed pilot
closed-loop navigation), and an exactly audited 96,320-job benchmark of 43
neural configurations across four public cohorts and one local cohort. Its
innovation is twofold: coordinate-field networks that parameterize spatial
weights over electrode coordinates rather than channel indices, whose compact
continuations were within descriptive uncertainty of a recent transformer
comparator (TCFormer) at 42% and 18% of its parameters and an order of
magnitude lower latency; and a fully reproducible,
participant-aware evaluation procedure with frozen seeds and per-job audit.
The work is significant for biomedical engineering because it demonstrates an
auditable, resource-realistic path from electrode to assistive command. The
manuscript reports observed rankings as development evidence with explicit
uncertainty, not as proof of global superiority or clinical benefit.
